# Reach, touch controls: critique

Date: 2026-11-14. Branch `feat/reach` at `d98fcba`. Documents: `2026-11-14-reach-touch-design.md` ("the design") and `../plans/2026-11-14-reach-touch-plan.md` ("the plan"). An independent check: the reviewer did not write either document and took no claim on trust. Line numbers are at `d98fcba` (the code is the same as at `15b3667`).

Nothing was run: no test, no build, no dev server, no browser, no device. Evidence is the code, the three reference designs (Stage 3, 4 and 5) and the platform rules as published. Where a platform claim cannot be settled by reading, it is marked **device** and was routed to the device checklist or to the new early device step H-0.

## Verdict

**READY-WITH-FIXES.** The direction is right: tap-to-act, Pointer Events beside the frozen mouse code, pure recognisers, the warning arithmetic and the "measure, then 15% for everyone" rule are sound, and the reaction-time sums check out to the millisecond. But one premise of the whole stage was never tested (can a phone run this game at all?), several claims that a goal rests on are false against the code (the hero stays visible with the backpack open; the desktop is unchanged at 1280 by 720; the long press calls "the same list builder"), and the plan's order shows nothing on a phone until every lane has merged. All of it was fixable in the documents; the fixes are applied. The owner decisions are listed at the end, with what changed in them.

## Findings

Each finding gives the evidence, the fix, and whether the fix is applied (all applied unless marked "owner").

### Critical

**C1. Nothing measures a phone before the stage is built, and the designed levers miss the likely bottleneck.** The phone's frame budget (24 ms on Low, 33 ms after the last step, design 10.3) has no estimate behind it. The only real-hardware figure is the owner's RTX 4070 desktop at 51 FPS average (about 20 ms); which build, tier and resolution it was taken on is not recorded in any document read. The build of this tree draws 1,509 calls at the start pose (Stage 5 section 1, fact 5: "draw calls, not triangles, are what the world spends"). Stage 5's Low tier aims at no more than 1,000 calls. A phone browser pays several times a desktop's CPU cost per draw call. If the frame is bound by draw calls, the two levers the design has (drop the pixel floor, then a 30-frame cap) do not touch it. The first time anyone measures is G-2 item 18, after every task. *Fix (applied):* new step **H-0** right after R0: the owner opens the deployed post-Stage-5 build on the reference phone, picks Low, and reads the frame time and draw calls at the start town for 60 s. This needs no Stage 7 code, because taps already walk by accident and Settings is reachable. H-0 reads upright and sideways (sideways is the widest view, the worst case for calls). It is optimistic by construction: it runs before Stage 7 raises the pixel count about threefold and widens the upright view, so a pass shows only that the draw-call side is fine; G-2 item 18 still judges the finished stage. A stated go/no-go follows: above 33 ms, the stage returns to the owner before the lanes start, with the levers that do cut calls (shorter draw distance or fog end on phones; scatter off) named as options. Design 10.3 and 15 (K6) and plan 0.5, 0.6 and section 1 are amended.

### High

**H1. The smallest promised screen does not fit, and its numbers are not honest.** At 360 by 560 the backpack sheet cannot hold 7 rows of 44 px in 52% of the height (308 > 291). The top block (about 130 px), three chat lines (about 54 px), the sheet and the 48 px dock leave about 17 px of world. The sizes "360 by 560" and "640 by 320" are stated as what small phones show, but a 360 by 640 Android phone with the browser's address bar and the system bars may show nearer 512 px upright and under 300 px sideways (estimate: **device**). A 344 px wide foldable cover screen is also under the line. *Fix (applied):* the minimum is restated as "to be measured in H-0 on the owner's phone, provisionally 360 by 512 and 568 by 280". The chat strip hides while the panel sheet is open. Where 7 rows do not fit (any viewport under 700 px tall), the upright backpack is drawn 7 across and 4 down. Whether to use 7 by 4 on every phone is added to CALL-7.5 (owner).

**H2. The hero is under the open sheet.** The camera looks at the hero's head (`loop.ts` 535-537), so the hero is at the vertical centre of the screen, not "in the upper half" (design 7.2). A sheet of 52% plus the dock covers it, and the warning circle under it, which is exactly where eating in a fight happens. On a sideways phone the 336 px sheet covers the horizontal centre of a 640 px screen. *Fix (applied, inside owner call CALL-7.7):* while a panel sheet is open on a phone, the projection centre is shifted to the middle of the uncovered area with `camera.setViewOffset`. The camera does not move. A tap's ray and the overlay's projection both go through the same projection matrix (`pointerToWorld` 245-253, `worldToScreenPos` `overlay.ts` 137-144), so picking stays correct. It is a pure function `viewShift(viewport, covered)`, tested, and wired after the lanes merge (task V5).

**H3. The on-screen keyboard can switch the layout.** Design 7.1 says height-only queries keep a keyboard from being "mistaken for a rotation". They do the opposite: in a browser that shrinks the layout for the keyboard, an upright phone typing in the bank search falls under `max-height: 499px` and gets the sideways layout. The design's section 16 already suspects this. *Fix (applied):* the viewport tag gains `interactive-widget=resizes-visual`, the setting that keeps the keyboard from resizing the layout in browsers that read it (Chrome and Firefox; **device**). The scan test asserts it, and checklist item 9 checks it. The false sentence is replaced.

**H4. The desktop changes at 1280 by 720.** "In the wide layout under 740 px of height the minimap is drawn at 150 px" (design 7.4) sits outside `html[data-input="touch"]`, and plan L1 accepts that query as a top-level block. 720 is under 740, and a 1366 by 768 laptop's browser shows about 650. G7.9 breaks. *Fix (applied):* the rule is scoped to `html[data-input="touch"]`, and L1 no longer accepts a bare wide-and-short query.

**H5. There is no options-list builder for the long press to call.** The world's options list is built inline in the right-click handler `onGameContextMenu` (`input.ts` 255-350). That handler takes a `MouseEvent`, cancels the event, returns when `G.rightPress.dist > 6` (a value only the mouse resets, so a hybrid device can carry a stale one), and after T5 returns for touch. The plan's S1 and the design's 6.4 rely on a builder that does not exist. Also, the long press used the exact ray only, so on a phone, where targets are 16 to 22 px, a long press on a monster mostly opened the ground's menu. *Fix (applied):* the body after the guards moves into `openWorldOptions(clientX, clientY, picked)`. `onGameContextMenu` keeps its guards and calls it with `pointerToWorld`'s result, which is exactly today's behaviour. The long press calls it with the same pick a tap uses (exact hit, then tolerance). It is added to the list of 6.1 and to T5 and S1, and Stage 4's vow entry (R4.3) goes in that function.

**H6. "One tap, one order" rested on one unverified browser behaviour, and its fall-back was outside the allowed edits.** The double walk order and the stuck tooltip are prevented only by `preventDefault()` on the touch `pointerdown`. Whether WebKit honours that for the made-up mouse events is listed in the design's own section 16 as unknown. The fall-back named there ("the mouse listeners ignore events within half a second of a finger") is an edit to the mouse listeners that rule 0.3.4 forbids. *Fix (applied):* two one-line guards become part of the allowed list. The canvas `mousedown`'s left-button branch (115-128) and the hover branch of `mousemove` (140-142) return when `lastPointer()` is `'touch'`. `inputMode.ts` also records `pointermove` from a mouse, so a real mouse is always recognised before its own mouse event, and a hybrid's hover comes back as soon as the mouse moves. This holds whatever WebKit does. `preventDefault()` stays as the first line of defence.

**H7. A resting thumb or a palm blocks every tap.** The recogniser's `spent` phase waits "until every finger is up" (design 6.2, T2). A thumb resting on the world fires a long press at 500 ms, goes `spent`, and from then on every tap of the other thumb is swallowed until the resting thumb lifts. A grip finger on a tablet's edge does the same. *Fix (applied):* a pointer that is still down after its gesture ended is **parked**. In `spent`, a new `down` starts a fresh press for the new finger and the parked ones are ignored until they lift. Pinch is unchanged. T1 gains the sequence; checklist item 5 gains "with the other thumb resting on the screen". Ignoring touches that start near the screen edge was considered and left as a device-check remedy, because it would also ignore taps on monsters drawn there.

**H8. The save import accepts anything shaped like an object, and the backup cannot be restored.** `migrateLegacySave` turns `{}` or `{"a":1}` into a fresh level-1 character with `corrupt: false` (`save.ts` 31-32). So `importSaveText` as specified would offer to replace the player's character with nothing. The "one-slot backup" has no way back from the interface. Pasting a huge text into the box costs before any check runs. *Fix (applied):* the import refuses a parsed value unless `game` is "Gielinor Hero" and `version` is a whole number from 1 to the current version (what `serializeState` writes first, 139-140, and what the hostile fixture also carries, so the existing fixture still reaches the reader). The paste box has a `maxlength` one above the limit, so an oversized paste arrives cut off and is refused. The Move window offers "Put back the character replaced on <date>" when a backup exists, which is an import of the backup through the same path. X1 gains these cases.

**H9. The reaction log does not say what it times, and it would count the wrong things.** "How long after its start the first walk order arrived" names no clock and no event. It counts warnings where the hero was outside the shape or already walking (an answer that needs no tap), attack orders, and mouse answers on a tablet with a mouse. It scores "no answer" as "not inside", so a ranged hero standing clear fails the pass rule by doing the right thing. *Fix (applied):* start is the frame timestamp of the first frame that drew the wind-up; the answer is the `timeStamp` of the input event that produced the first accepted order of any kind that moves the hero (same clock: both are on the page's high-resolution timeline). A warning counts only if the hero stood inside the shape at its start with no walk in progress. Each answer records its pointer type, and the summary is per pointer type. The interval covers display, person, contact and touch panel, and not the frames after the order, which is what the allowance leaves for the person. W1's contract is updated.

**H10. Measuring on the phone silently depends on CALL-7.11.** The performance readout on a phone and the reaction lines appear only while the hidden tools are revealed (design 7.2, 9.2 step 2, 10.3). On a phone that needs CALL-7.11. If the owner says no, G-2 item 18 and G-3 can be read only over a USB cable with a desktop inspector, and the plan's "if no" row does not say so. *Fix (applied):* the readout and the reaction lines also show when the page is opened with `?measure` in its address. The flag stores nothing and gives no tool or booster. It is read once at boot. CALL-7.11 no longer carries the measurement.

**H11. The fat-finger rule picks the wrong thing in the cases it exists for.** (a) Crowd: on a sideways phone, goblins 16 to 24 px wide get 48 px circles, which cover the ground between them, so "tap the ground between goblins" attacks one. The design's own checklist item 3 expects the opposite. (b) Far things: a monster 5 px wide on the horizon gets a 48 px target, so a long walk order becomes an attack. (c) Occlusion: a circle drawn over a hill picks a monster behind it. (d) Stations are not cylinders (`worldBuild.ts` 189, 330, 386, 435), so "the cylinder's radius" does not exist for them. *Fix (applied, within CALL-7.4):* tolerance picks a thing only when **exactly one** enlarged circle contains the tap; zero or several give the ground (a walk is the safe mistake), which also removes the tie order. A thing drawn with a radius under 5 px gets no enlargement (5, not 8: at the provisional sideways minimum of 280 px a person at the farthest zoom has a radius of about 6.4 px). The one picked must be visible: a ray from the camera to its centre must not meet the ground first, checked in the engine for that one candidate. The radius is the cylinder's where the hit volume is a cylinder, otherwise half the larger horizontal side of its bounding box. T3 and T4 contracts are updated.

**H12. The plan shows nothing on a phone until the end, and its lanes collide.** No slice is playable on a phone before all three lanes merge. `touch-action` (L2, layout lane) is what lets T6's drag and pinch reach the game at all, so T6 cannot pass its browser check inside its own lane. V4 "starts after L2 has merged", but the layout lane merges only at the end. S2, L4 and V4 each insert one line at the same place in `boot`, which git reports as a conflict. *Fix (applied):* the platform rules (viewport tag, `touch-action`, overscroll, callout, `dvh`) and their scan test move into the spine before the lanes are cut (tasks L1 and L2 become spine tasks). The layout half of the scan becomes its own test, L2b, at the start of the layout lane, so no branch carries a red test it cannot fix. A first merge point, **slice A**, comes after T6: the touch lane merges, a preview build goes to the owner, and **H-1** checks items 1 to 6 of the checklist on the phone while the other lanes continue. The long-press watcher is started from `attachTouchInput`, the dock from `main.ts`, and the cards and V4 move after the merge, so only L7 and the post-merge tasks edit `runtime.ts`.

### Medium

**M1. The synthetic-pointer fall-back proves less than the plan says.** Untrusted `PointerEvent`s make no compatibility mouse events, no `click`, no `contextmenu`, and no `touch-action` effect. "One order per tap", "no stuck tooltip", "a long press does not use the item" and "no system menu" would pass without being tested. `setPointerCapture` throws for a pointer id that is not active. *Fix (applied):* PRE-1 prefers trusted touch through the browser's debugging protocol. Under the synthetic fall-back, P1 lists which assertions are real (recogniser wiring, layout, measurements). The others go to the device checklist by name. The recipe shims `setPointerCapture` on the page, not in product code.

**M2. The sell question misses the one-tap bulk sales, and a number is wrong.** "Sell all gear" (`stations.ts` 357, `sellAllUnequippedGear` via `runtime.ts` 136) and the quick "all" on a sell card (401-405) sell many pieces with one tap. Design 6.5 says the shop sells at "ten times what it pays". Stage 3 buys at 10 times list and sells back at 0.65, so the spread is about 15 to 1, as CALL-7.10 says. *Fix (applied):* any sale started by touch that includes equipment asks once, with the count and the total. 6.5 corrected.

**M3. The long-press click swallow can eat the wrong click.** "Removed after 700 ms" from an unstated moment. A browser may not send a click after a long press at all, and then the next real tap (often the first sheet row) is eaten. *Fix (applied):* the swallow is armed on the lift of the long-pressed finger, eats only a click whose target is inside the pressed element, and expires 400 ms after the lift.

**M4. Inline styles beat the sheet's CSS.** `openChooseMenu` sets `left` and `top` inline (`input.ts` 368-371). A stylesheet rule cannot override them without `!important`. *Fix (applied):* L3 says so.

**M5. Android's long-press menu on elements with no handler.** Only three handlers cancel `contextmenu`. Bank and shop stacks, tabs and buttons do not, and Android fires `contextmenu` on a long press anywhere. *Fix (applied):* the page-level long-press watcher cancels `contextmenu` while `lastPointer()` is `'touch'`, except in text boxes.

**M6. The touch drag shares `camState.isDragging` with the mouse listeners.** The window `mouseup` clears it (`input.ts` 145-147) and the window `mousemove` reads it with a stale `lastMouseX` (131-139). On a hybrid, or with any stray made-up mouse event, the camera jumps or the drag is cut. *Fix (applied):* the touch drag also writes `camState.lastMouseX` and `lastMouseY` on every move, so a stray mouse move turns the camera by a pixel at most. No edit to the mouse code.

**M7. A tap with the second thumb may not click.** Browsers synthesise `click` from a single-finger tap. A tap on a backpack slot while the other thumb is still dragging the camera may never click (**device**). *Fix (applied):* checklist item 13 tests it. The fall-back is named: the page-level recogniser, which already sees every pointer, clicks the element on a recognised tap from a non-primary finger.

**M8. The device table is wrong at two edges.** An iPad mini held upright is 744 px wide, so it gets the phone layout, not "today's layout". A foldable's cover screen is 344 px wide, under the promised minimum. *Fix (applied):* the table names both. The iPad mini case is acceptable as it stands (dock and sheet suit a small tablet), and the FOV rule gives it 53°.

**M9. The budgets read a frame interval, not a frame's cost.** The readout is `elapsed / frames` (`loop.ts` 760-761). Under the 60-frame cap it never shows under 16.7 ms, so "goal 16.7 ms" cannot be measured. On a 90 Hz screen the cap gives 22.2 ms intervals, 1.8 ms under Stage 5's 24 ms step-down line. *Fix (applied):* 10.3 says what is read. The heat test is restated as "still at the cap at minute 20". The work time, where wanted, comes from Stage 5's `look.bench`.

**M10. CALL-7.16 understates its cost.** The graphics tier is not saved (Stage 5 9, "The graphics tier is not saved, as today"), so a strong phone starts on Low **every time the game is opened**, not once. *Fix (applied):* stated in 10.1 and CALL-7.16.

**M11. CALL-7.15 is missing its cheapest option, and its pass rule is new.** Acting on contact only while a warning aimed at the hero is live recovers the 100 ms contact term exactly when it matters. With +50 ms instead of +150 every row keeps its 200 ms reserve except Ember Cone (199 ms). The cost: a drag or pinch begun during a warning starts with a walk order to where the finger landed, which is open ground the player chose. The pass rule (9 of 10 cleared, three of four inside the allowance) is also new: Stage 4's trackpad check (its G-8, CALL-4.16) states none. *Fix (owner):* added as option (c) of CALL-7.15, with the numbers. The design notes that the trackpad check can use the same log and rule.

**M12. Plan allowlists miss Stage 5's tier code.** Stage 5 puts the tier governor and the pixel budget in `src/engine/post.ts` (Stage 5 design, file table). V3 lists only `renderer.ts` and `loop.ts`. *Fix (applied):* V3 lists "the file R0 names for Stage 5's tier choice, pixel budget and governor (`post.ts` in Stage 5's design)".

**M13. A name collision.** Stage 4 adds `src/engine/reactions.ts` (hit reactions). W2 adds `src/state/reactions.ts`. *Fix (applied):* renamed `src/state/reactionLog.ts`.

**M14. The dock has no words.** The six tab buttons are icons with a `title` only (`index.html` 179-184). The design's sketch shows labels, but nothing specifies them. A touch player sees seven unexplained icons. *Fix (applied):* L4 adds a short visible label to each tab in the phone layouts.

**M15. The sideways sheet must cover the minimap.** At 320 px or less of height, a 4-row backpack (176 px) cannot sit between a 96 px minimap and the dock. *Fix (applied):* 7.3 says the open sheet runs from the top safe area to the dock and covers the minimap while open.

## Checks that found nothing wrong

- Thresholds. 10 px slop and 500 ms hold match the platform conventions (Android's slop is 8 dp; iOS's long-press default is 0.5 s; Android has used a shorter long press on recent versions, which the `contextmenu` guard makes harmless). 8 px for the pinch start is under the slop, so a pinch never reads as a drag first. Whether 10 px survives a hurried thumb is on the checklist (K4); the cost is asymmetric (a lost dodge against a small camera turn), and the constant is one number.
- Pointer Events against Touch Events; the single non-passive act; no `preventDefault` on a move. Pinch blocking by `touch-action` on the root and on the canvas holds on Chromium. On an iPhone it is unverified, with the `gesturestart` fall-back in K1.
- Audio. A touch's `pointerdown` does not count as a user gesture for audio in the HTML rules; its `pointerup` and `touchend` do. So R4.4 is right, and stronger than "an iPhone" alone.
- Safe areas need `viewport-fit=cover`; `100dvh` with a `100vh` fall-back; 16 px text boxes. All correct.
- The FOV formula: 50° at aspect 0.78 and above; 76.6° at 0.462, clamped to 75 (39° across); about 52° at 0.75.
- The save transfer reuses the validating reader. Every id is checked against the tables, every number is clamped, and the result is built from a fresh default, so no pasted key reaches an object's prototype (with the shape gate of H8).
- **Desktop parity.** Jump and held-key walking have no touch form, by an owner call (CALL-7.2); no rule reads either. The special attack is a tap on its bar; prayers and eating are a dock tab and a slot (two taps, then one while the panel stays open), and with the view shift the hero stays visible meanwhile. Q and E are the drag, F1 to F6 the dock, Esc the close buttons. Typed words remain keyboard-only, by Stage 3's accepted limit (CALL-7.11).
- **Pause card, context loss, memory.** The pause card is justified: the loop already freezes the world while hidden (`loop.ts` 774 caps the step), so the card adds no rule, only the moment to find one's thumb before a fight resumes; desktop is untouched. The reload card on context loss and the save on `pagehide` are the simple, certain recoveries. Memory is measured as a flat `renderer.info.memory` over 30 minutes, which Stage 1 already defined.
